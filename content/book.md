## Foreword by Dr Anand Deshpande

**Founder, Chairman & MD, Persistent Systems**

Dr Anand Deshpande founded Persistent Systems in 1990. He holds a B.Tech from IIT Kharagpur and a PhD from Indiana University. A technology visionary, he champions entrepreneurship, education, and data initiatives across India.

I am delighted that Rengan Jayakrishnan has written this wonderful book, The Sales Algorithm. I met Rengan recently through my cousin, who was his classmate at IIT Roorkee. During our first conversation, and later through his podcast, I realised the depth and breadth of his sales experience. Over the last 30 years, he has accumulated a body of practical wisdom, rarely seen written down so clearly. With this book, he has done a remarkable job of distilling that experience into a highly usable handbook, one that will serve all those building or being part of a sales organisation.

In my own work with entrepreneurs, I have repeatedly seen how central the sales function is to a business's health, confidence and future. Many founders treat the sales process as a black box and expect that a bluebird will deliver that big deal. What we call "sales success" is often the result of structured thinking, clear playbooks, disciplined pipelines, and repeatable processes. When those are absent, companies struggle. When they are present, companies scale. Yet, despite its importance, sales remain the most misunderstood capability in our entrepreneurial ecosystem.

At Persistent and in my work with Dr Ashok Korwar, we have long believed that business growth does not follow a smooth, linear curve. Instead, it moves in a series of S-curves — or what we call orbits. Each orbit represents a phase of growth with its own rules, challenges, and demands. The transition from the first orbit to the second orbit is often traumatic, challenging, and complex, but essential. Inability to make this transition inevitably results in a gradual, and sometimes irreversible, decline.

What makes this shift so tricky is that what works in one orbit rarely works in the next. The assumptions that carried a company through the first S-curve begin to fail. Strategies that once made perfect sense stop producing results. As you scale, the customers you call on change, the nature of deals changes, the kind of talent you need changes, and organisational dynamics change. Leaders and teams who were highly effective in the first orbit suddenly find the ground shifting beneath their feet. Unless new muscles are built deliberately, the business begins to stall.

Over the last fifteen years, through our Second Orbit programme, Dr Korwar and I have worked with hundreds of entrepreneurs and heard from more than a thousand companies. Across industries, geographies, and business models, one challenge consistently stands out as the single biggest impediment to scaling: the inability to build a robust, repeatable sales machine.

In the first orbit, the founder must be the primary salesperson. They must rely on their personal networks, credibility, goodwill, and sheer persistence to close the early deals. They lean on trust, often built over years, to get the first customers to bet on them. They work closely with these early adopters to refine the offering and achieve product-market fit. It is a heroic, exhausting phase, and typically, only one person can carry the business through it: the founder.

But inevitably, the founder hits a ceiling. Time, attention, relationships, and personal bandwidth do not scale. If the founder remains the single critical salesperson, the business will not be able to find its next S-curve. This is the moment when the transition to the second orbit becomes unavoidable.

In the second orbit, the business must consciously invest in systems, processes, and structure. Sales must shift from a personality-driven to a process-driven approach. The organisation needs a well-defined offering, a disciplined pipeline, clarity on buyer personas, a structured discovery and qualification process, and a standardised approach to deal closure. Sales cannot be an act of improvisation. It must become a teachable, learnable, repeatable capability.

And this is precisely why Rengan's book is so timely and so useful.

The Sales Algorithm brings structure to an area that many professionals experience, but few can articulate. The frameworks in this book — from the basics of B2B sales cycles to the nuances of selling to Fortune 500 companies — help readers build the confidence and clarity they need to operate in larger, more sophisticated environments. Concepts such as top-down selling, blended approaches, crafting a sales playbook, managing champions, and qualifying opportunities are explained with simplicity and backed by real stories.

One of the aspects I appreciate most is that the book is grounded in the Indian context. While global sales literature is extensive, very little of it speaks to the realities of Indian founders selling to Indian enterprises, or Indian teams selling to global companies. The cultural nuances, the long sales cycles, the need for early adopters, and the importance of credibility markers are all captured thoughtfully here.

What also stands out is the workbook-style design of this book. The "Try it out" sections at the end of each chapter convert theory into action. They nudge the reader not just to understand the ideas but to practise them, mapping stakeholders, crafting value propositions, refining elevator pitches, and organising their funnels. In a field like sales, where improvement comes through repetition and reflection, these exercises are immensely helpful.

The truth is that sales isn't taught in college. Most engineers, technologists, and founders enter the market without any grounding in enterprise selling. Even experienced mid-career professionals often find that their skills plateau when they attempt to move from user-level selling to CXO-level engagement. The result is a shortage of confident, well-trained sales professionals at a time when India needs them the most.

Rengan's contribution, therefore, is significant. He has created a handbook not just for sales teams, but for founders, senior leaders, and anyone interested in developing a systematic approach to customer acquisition and revenue growth. His extensive global experience shows through in the nuance and pragmatism of his recommendations. The book does not promise shortcuts; instead, it equips readers with the tools and habits that enable long-term excellence.

This book is the kind one keeps referring to. Not because it is complex, but because it is layered, and different chapters become relevant at various stages of a company's journey. For a founder seeking product-market fit, a sales manager looking to break into enterprise accounts, or a business preparing for the second orbit, the lessons in these pages will bring clarity and confidence.

I am confident that The Sales Algorithm will be a "must-keep" reference for many readers. I wish Rengan and the book every success and hope it finds its way into the hands of entrepreneurs and sales leaders across the country.

## Foreword by Dr Ashok Korwar

**Former Professor, IIM Ahmedabad**

Dr Ashok Korwar holds a B.Tech from IIT Bombay and a PhD from UCLA. A former IIM Ahmedabad professor, he has advised Dr Anand Deshpande at Persistent Systems for 15 years and created the Second Orbit programme, helping startups transition to scalable, sustainable growth.

I have taught at leading management schools for 45 years now and have never seen a course on Sales! Marketing, yes, but not Sales. Sales is a practitioner's world, a world of 'doers'. There is little 'intellectual content' to sales, so it is difficult to develop a framework for it. After all, every sales situation is different.

However, there are certain principles and patterns that can be articulated, and this book does precisely that. That is its value. Do you need a primer to get your new sales manager started? A coaching manual for your new salespeople? This book is that.

The Sales Algorithm emphasises the very real need to have a sales team in the first place and the need to develop and use a playbook. Topics of great importance are covered here, including how to manage accounts, partners, and sales leaders.

Sales is the single most important function in any company, because, without sales, you have nothing. This book helps you get started and keep improving in a systematic, disciplined way.

## Afterword by Sandip Chintawar

**IIT Roorkee Alumnus, Entrepreneur and Investor**

It was January 2025, and I was on holiday with my family in Amritsar when my phone rang. On the other end was Rengan Jayakrishnan — JK, as I have known him since our M.Tech days at IIT Roorkee. He told me he was writing a book on sales and wanted to interview me for a podcast, which he planned to host, drawing on my real-life experiences as an entrepreneur. I agreed immediately...

## My Journey with My Book

From early January 2025, what began as an idea gradually took shape into this book. My wife, Jayashree, constantly encouraged me with "out-of-the-box" ideas that helped move the project forward. When writing felt overwhelming, my elder son, Kshitij, suggested journaling my sales coaching sessions — this became the backbone of the content. My daughter-in-law, Baani, advised me to enrich the book with real-life experiences, which brought depth and relatability. My younger son, Shashvat, proposed the title The Sales Algorithm and the idea of adding "Try It Out" sections, shaping the book into a practical, workbook-style guide.

A major turning point came with the launch of The Sales Algorithm Podcast. My first guest, Sandip Chintawar — friend, entrepreneur, and investor — set the tone for what followed. His support was instrumental. Soon after, Aatul Wadegaonkar provided invaluable feedback on early drafts, strengthening the foundation of the book.

Another defining moment was the opportunity to interview Anand Deshpande, facilitated by Makarand Deshpande. Anand's insights were deeply aligned with the book's core ideas and added immense value. Conversations with other founders like Kshiteesh Deshmukh, Kaustubh Deshmukh, and Sunil Jalihal further enriched the narrative.

Expanding the format, I hosted panel discussions featuring Ashok Korwar and Ram Pazhayannur, and later on AI in sales and marketing with Sandip Chintawar and Udit Agarwal. These sessions, hosted at Wai Technologies' studio with support from Omkar Choudhari, added new dimensions to the book.

I am grateful for the forewords by Anand Deshpande and Ashok Korwar, and the afterword by Sandip Chintawar. This journey has been deeply fulfilling, and I hope this book helps sales leaders, managers, and founders turn ideas into actionable outcomes.
